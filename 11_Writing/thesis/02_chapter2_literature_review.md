# Chapter 2: Systematic Literature Review and Gap Assessment (`WRIT0003`)

## 2.1 Search Strategy, Screening Architecture and Corpus Independence

To ground our methodology in verifiable full-text evidence rather than unverified citation chains, we conducted a structured two-batch literature harvest (`SEARCH0001` and `SEARCH0002`, cutoff `2026-11-16`) that yielded `180` candidate PDFs, reduced after exact cross-batch deduplication to **`122` unique records (`P0001–P0122`)** reconciled one-to-one with `02_Literature/references.bib` (111 verified via Crossref DOI API + 11 verified via primary PDF page-1 metadata). Per `DEC0019` (Option 1, `G03 PASS`), all deleted historical title-phase seed references were formally excluded from evidential support; every literature claim in this thesis (`CLAIM0001–CLAIM0012`) traces strictly to verified full-text extractions:

- **Core Group A (`23` studies: `P0001, P0003–P0009, P0011–P0025`, years `2023–2026`):** Direct 2D OPG bounding-box or landmark object detectors extracted in full (`T05` tables).
- **Core Group B (`19` studies: `P0002, P0010, P0026–P0042`, years `2022–2026`):** Contextual and methodological studies encompassing `4` systematic/narrative reviews [Hartman2026_P0027; Lutescu2026_P0028; BonfantiGris2025_P0034; Demir2024_P0037], paired 2D OPG vs. 3D CBCT/bitewing physical-limit studies [Kazimierczak2024_P0039; vanNistelrooij2026_P0032; PornprasertsukDamrongsri2025_P0036], domain-shift and enhancement ablations [Nayak2026_P0029; NISHIMURA2026_P0030], semantic segmentation comparators [Durmu2025_P0035; Yilmaz2026_P0033; Panetta2022_P0042], MLLM benchmarks [Peng2026_P0031], and two-stage frameworks [AlabdAljabar2026_P0026; Zhou2024_P0040].
- **Reserve (`55` records: `P0043–P0097`) & Excluded (`25` records: `P0098–P0122`):** Screened at title/abstract level (`27/55` Reserve records also apply YOLO to dental imaging, reinforcing that multi-class OPG detection is active globally; `25` records were excluded for out-of-scope forensic/non-detection tasks, non-OPG modalities such as CBCT/BW/PA/intraoral photos, missing full text, or withdrawal).

Importantly, several Core papers share underlying public cohorts—including `UMFIH` (`1,628` internal + `180` external Romanian OPGs in [Mureanu2024_P0023] and [Eftimie2025_P0015]), `Do et al. (2024)` (`PAI 3–5` periapical lesions in [Oztekin2026_P0008] and [Scarano2026_P0011]), the `407`-OPG impacted-tooth benchmark ([Kk2025_P0018] vs. [Durmu2025_P0035]), `Tufts` [Panetta2022_P0042], and `DENTEX` ([Dehghani2025_P0014] and [PornprasertsukDamrongsri2025_P0036]). Tracking shared clusters prevents double-counting overlapping cohorts as independent validation. Summary census metrics across the `42` Core studies are compiled in Table `TAB0001`.

## 2.2 Multi-Class 2D OPG Object Detection and the Contrast-Tier Disparity

Thirteen of the `23` Group A detectors target `4–13` classes simultaneously—including `13` pre-radiotherapy conditions on `UMFIH` (`YOLOv8x`, internal `mAP@0.50 = 0.600`, external `0.451` [Mureanu2024_P0023]; `YOLO11m` `0.742` internal vs. `0.537` external [Eftimie2025_P0015]), `11` classes via `RCTE-Net` (`mAP@0.50 = 0.571`, `mAP@0.50:0.95 = 0.328` [Peng2026_P0009]), `12` restorative/anatomical targets (`YOLOv7m` `0.740` / `0.481` [BonfantiGris2024_P0022]), `10` classes (`YOLO26m` `0.87–0.98` on positive subsets [BaseriSaadi2026_P0001]), `6` Dhaka handheld-photographed classes (`YOLOv7` `0.9354` on `23` test OPGs without minority per-class AP [Rahman2024_P0024]), and `4` `DENTEX` disease classes (`YOLOv11` mean `0.504` / `0.326` across `Table III` [Dehghani2025_P0014]).

This directly **refutes** the broad hypothesis (`GAP0001`) that multi-class OPG detection does not exist (`CLAIM0001`). However, inspecting per-class breakdowns exposes a consistent **radiographic contrast hierarchy (`CLAIM0002`)**: dense metallic or large structural targets regularly exceed `0.83–0.98` `AP@0.50` (implants, bridges, crowns, impacted teeth), whereas small low-contrast radiolucencies and diffuse alveolar defects collapse sharply—such as periapical lesions (`0.0726` in [Peng2026_P0009], `0.315` in [Mureanu2024_P0023], `0.322` in [Dehghani2025_P0014]), carious lesions (`0.2497` in [Peng2026_P0009], `0.373` in [Mureanu2024_P0023], `0.403` in [Dehghani2025_P0014]), diffuse bone loss (`0.154–0.165` [Mureanu2024_P0023]), and rare anomalies (`0.000` supernumerary in [BonfantiGris2024_P0022]). Frontier zero-shot multimodal LLMs (`M365, Gemini, Qwen3-VL`) perform even worse on whole-OPG localization (`Caries <= 0.075`, `Periapical <= 0.023`, `Bone loss = 0.000` [Peng2026_P0031]), underscoring the continued necessity of specialized spatial detectors.

## 2.3 Representation Tradeoffs: Bounding Boxes vs. Segmentation and Modality Limits

Controlled comparisons on identical cohorts show that **bounding-box detection outperforms semantic segmentation for discrete multi-instance screening (`CLAIM0005`, `GAP0003`)**: on the exact same `407` OPGs (`325/21/61` split), a `YOLOv8 + RT-DETR + WBF` bounding-box ensemble achieved `96.0% F1` and `98.3% mAP@0.50` [Kk2025_P0018], outperforming `40` semantic segmentation runs (`U-Net + EfficientNetB7`, `92.02% F1`, `85.29% IoU`) that merged touching impactions [Durmu2025_P0035]. Similarly, bounding-box `YOLOv7m` (`0.740` / `0.481`) outperformed instance-segmentation `YOLOv8m` (`0.591` / `0.272`) on `12` classes [BonfantiGris2024_P0022], and `YOLOv11` (`mAP@0.50 = 0.872`) outperformed `Mask R-CNN` (`0.787`) and `DeepLabV3` (`0.617`) on pediatric teeth [Ozcelik2026_P0007]. Nevertheless, `AABB` representations have documented weaknesses on continuous root-length morphometry (where keypoints achieved `0.88 F1` vs. `0.60` for `AABB` [zden2026_P0013]), oblique teeth (`OBB` gained `+4.1%` over `AABB` [Khurshid2026_P0006]), and diffuse bone loss [Mureanu2024_P0023]. Furthermore, cascading tight tooth crops before lesion detection clips `9.7%` of periapical lesions [Scarano2026_P0011] and reduces apical sensitivity from `0.854` to `0.538` [vanNistelrooij2026_P0032].

Across modalities (`GAP0004`, `CLAIM0006`), paired `OPG + CBCT` evaluation proved that 2D OPG suffers intrinsic projective visibility ceilings (`33.33%` sensitivity on OPG vs. `77.78%` on CBCT [Kazimierczak2024_P0039]), while same-day bitewing studies cataloged eight anatomical OPG mimics (cervical burnout, pulp horns, palatoglossal air space) that drive false positives [PornprasertsukDamrongsri2025_P0036] and confirmed initial non-cavitated enamel caries cannot be reliably detected on 2D OPG [vanNistelrooij2026_P0032]. Within 2D OPG itself (`CLAIM0007`), external cross-scanner validation consistently degrades accuracy by `-9.0%` to `-22.2%` `mAP@0.50` [Eftimie2025_P0015; Mureanu2024_P0023; evik2025_P0016; Nayak2026_P0029] and can induce scanner-specific hallucinations such as bite blocks predicted as primary teeth [NISHIMURA2026_P0030].

## 2.4 Forensic Audit of Data Leakage, Metric Artifacts and the `CLAHE` Paradox

Our full-text forensic audit (`CLAIM0004`, `CLAIM0010`) revealed widespread methodological contamination that invalidates naive literature rankings:
- **Pre-Split Augmentation & Crop Leakage:** The sharpest proof appears on the identical Do (2024) periapical dataset: training on `17,004` pre-augmented copies yielded `mAP@0.50 = 0.9194` (`mAP@0.50:0.95 = 0.7794`) [Scarano2026_P0011], whereas removing duplicates to `3,924` unique OPGs before splitting dropped full-image `YOLO11m` to `0.528` and tiled `YOLO12m` to `0.615` (`+30.4%` leakage inflation [Oztekin2026_P0008]). Similar pre-split augmentation or crop-level leakage occurred in [Bhoopalan2026_P0002; Khurshid2026_P0006; Singh2025_P0021; RashidiRanjbar2023_P0025; PornprasertsukDamrongsri2025_P0036; Zhou2024_P0040].
- **Metric Omissions and Arithmetic Flaws:** Hartman et al. [Hartman2026_P0027] documented that `84.9%` (`62/73`) of dental YOLO studies omit `mAP@0.50:0.95` and `87.7%` (`64/73`) lack external validation. Individual papers additionally exhibited `Table II` vs. `Table III` contradictions [Dehghani2025_P0014], impossible `F1 > max(P, R)` prints [Ragab2025_P0020], cherry-picked multi-threshold metrics (`P=1.00 @ conf=0.905`, `R=0.96 @ conf=0.000` [RashidiRanjbar2023_P0025]), merged caries+fillings labels [evik2025_P0016], and ignored intra-OPG clustering that inflates naive standard errors by `3.05–6.17×` [Peng2026_P0031].
- **Preprocessing Paradox (`GAP0005`, `CLAIM0008–CLAIM0009`):** While [CH2026_P0004] reported gains from `CLAHE(clip=2)` + sharpening on cropped maxillary canines (`0.774 -> 0.948`), four controlled studies found `CLAHE` or enhancement *hurt* or failed to improve detection: `-3.91%` `mAP@0.50:0.95` in [Scarano2026_P0011], raw `Mask R-CNN` (`0.8173`) outperforming all `CLAHE` configurations (`0.8104–0.8151`) at `7.9×` lower latency in [Nayak2026_P0029], lower accuracy (`95.69%` vs. `97.50–99.17%`) in [Gwak2024_P0038], and explicit avoidance in [BaseriSaadi2026_P0001; Oztekin2026_P0008; Kaya2022_P0041]. Conversely, preserving spatial detail via native tiling (`+0.039` to `+0.121` `mAP@0.50` [Oztekin2026_P0008]) or higher input scales (`1280–1600px` [Eftimie2025_P0015; Scarano2026_P0011]) consistently benefited small targets without destroying intensity cues.

## 2.5 Inference Efficiency Reporting Deficit (`GAP0006`)

Across all `42` Core full-text studies, **`31/42` (`73.8%`) omit per-image inference latency (`ms/OPG` or `FPS`)**, including six papers that nonetheless claim `"real-time"` capability (`CLAIM0011`) [Khurshid2026_P0005; Khurshid2025_P0017; Kk2025_P0018; Peker2025_P0019; Yilmaz2026_P0033; Durmu2025_P0035]. Even when speed is reported, [Peng2026_P0009] omits its own defined end-to-end `FPS_all`, while high-recall pipelines slow dramatically to `0.30–6.29 OPG/s` [Oztekin2026_P0008; Scarano2026_P0011; Nayak2026_P0029] or `15 s/OPG` in cloud reporting [BaseriSaadi2026_P0001] (`CLAIM0012`). No Core study simultaneously delivers a deduplicated multi-cohort 2D OPG benchmark with `mAP@0.50`, `mAP@0.50:0.95`, `GroupID` cluster `95% CIs`, controlled `CLAHE` vs. `1280px` ablations, multi-country zero-shot shift testing, and matched GPU+CPU end-to-end (`T_pre + T_infer + T_post`) timing—defining the exact, bounded empirical contribution of Chapters 3–5.
