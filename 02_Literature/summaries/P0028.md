## 1. Problem & Clinical / Technical Motivation
- **Clinical & educational motivation (pp. 1–3, 8, 10):** Manual structured reporting of a single panoramic radiograph (`OPG`) can take up to **`15 minutes`** under clinical time pressure, leading to incomplete documentation and inter-observer disagreement (`Müller et al., 2021 [27]`, `p. 3`). Deep learning systems (`YOLO`, `RT-DETR`, `Mask R-CNN`, `U-Net`, `SAM2`) can automate tooth charting and pathology detection while serving as interactive training tools for dental students (`pp. 2, 10`).
- **Regulatory & translational gap (pp. 1, 7–9):** Technical accuracy alone is insufficient for clinical deployment; real-world adoption requires navigating dataset domain shift, clinician trust, patient informed consent, and Software as a Medical Device (`SaMD`) regulatory compliance in the **United States (`FDA`)** and **European Union (`EU MDR / AI Act`)**.
- **Objective (p. 2):** Provide a narrative synthesis of `2020–2025` OPG deep learning breakthroughs (`PubMed`, `Scopus`, `Google Scholar`; `61` references, `21` studies tabulated in `Table 1`, `pp. 6–7`) integrated with an analysis of US and EU legal, ethical, and regulatory frameworks (`Fig. 3`, p. 9).

## 2. Corpus Scope & Synthesized Evidence (`Sections 1–4`, `Table 1`, pp. 2–8)
- **Search scope (`Section 1`, p. 2):** Narrative literature search in **`PubMed`**, **`Scopus`**, and **`Google Scholar`** (`2020–2025`) using keywords `artificial intelligence`, `dentistry`, `panoramic radiograph`, `tooth detection`, and `diagnostic accuracy` (`no PRISMA flow counts reported`).
- **Key thematic findings across OPG tasks (`Sections 2–4, Table 1`, pp. 2–7):**
  1. **Reducing human error & inter-observer variability (`Sections 2.1, 2.3, 4.1`, pp. 2–3, 6–7):**
     - *Periapical lesions (`Agrawal & Nikhade, 2022 [58]` / `Ba-Hattab et al., 2023 [20]`):* On `1,040` teeth (`52` patients, CBCT reference), AI achieved `Accuracy = 88.2%, Sensitivity = 91.5%, Specificity = 83.3%`, with **AI-expert agreement (`Fleiss' κ = 0.83`) exceeding human inter-observer agreement (`κ = 0.71`)** (`p. 2, Table 1`). On `713` OPGs (`18,618` root regions, `Ba-Hattab et al., 2023 [20]`), two-stage `Faster R-CNN` (`AP = 74.95%`) + `Inception v3` (`84%` acc) achieved `Accuracy = 84.6%, Sensitivity = 72.2%, Specificity = 85.6%` (`pp. 3–4`).
     - *Periodontal bone loss (`Butnaru et al., 2025 [7]` & `AlGhaihab et al., 2025 [8]`):* Compared against `60` dentists on OPGs, AI achieved higher agreement with the gold standard on bone loss (**`κ = 0.76` vs. `κ = 0.57` for general dentists**, `pp. 2–3, Table 1`) and higher attachment-loss tooth counts (`mean score = 6.12` vs. `5.43` senior specialists, `4.58` specialists, `3.65` general dentists, `p. 6`). In `AlGhaihab et al., 2025 [8]` (`100` radiographs), AI assistance increased general dentists' diagnostic accuracy from **`73.6%` (unassisted) to `85.8%` (AI-assisted)** (`p. 3, Table 1`).
     - *Commercial `Diagnocat` endodontic evaluation (`55` patients, `pp. 2–3, Table 1`):* Achieved `Accuracy = 90.72%, F1 = 95.12%` for detecting the presence of endodontic fillings, but **dropped sharply on fine-grained quality metrics**: `Accuracy = 55.81%, F1 = 8.33%` for adequate obturation length (short fillings) and `Accuracy = 62.75%, F1 = 14.29%` for adequate density (voids).
     - *Population-scale epidemiological screening (`Turosz et al., 2024 [24]`, `p. 3`):* AI trained on `> 250,000` OPGs analyzed **`11,380` patients** in Kielce, Poland, automatically charting caries (`59.3%`), fillings (`76.2%`), missing teeth (`77.5%`), RCTs (`16.5%`), implants (`4.1%`), and crowns (`2.6%`).
  2. **YOLO, RT-DETR, Tiling, CLAHE, Super-Resolution & SAM2 (`Sections 2.2, 3.1–3.3, 4.2`, pp. 3–7):**
     - *Multi-center external domain shift (`Mureșanu et al., 2024 [28]` = our **`P0023`**):* `YOLOv8` trained on `1,628` internal OPGs (`P > 0.90, R > 0.80` on implants/endodontics/surgical devices) suffered a significant accuracy drop across `180` external multi-center OPGs (`p. 4, Table 1`).
     - *Hybrid CNN-Transformer (`Küçük et al., 2025 [38]` = our **`P0018`**):* `YOLOv8 + RT-DETR + WBF` on `407` OPGs achieved `mAP = 98.3%, F1 = 96%` (`p. 6, Table 1`).
     - *Third-molar surgical extraction difficulty (`Akdoğan et al., 2025 [10]`, `2,000` OPGs):* `YOLO11` sub-models automating the `Pederson Index` achieved `mAP = 0.975` (Winter's angulation, `YOLO11n`), `mAP = 0.965` (Pell & Gregory class, `YOLO11n`), `mAP = 0.989` (depth level, `YOLO11m`), and overall system **`Precision = 97.00%, Recall = 94.55%, F1 = 95.76%`** (`p. 6, Table 1`).
     - *Tiling for small restorations (`Rohrer et al., 2022 [30]`, `1,781` OPGs, `p. 5`):* Partitioning OPGs into rectangular tiles boosted `U-Net` restoration segmentation `F1` from **`0.70` (full OPG) -> `0.83` (`6` tiles) -> `0.92` (`10` tiles) -> `0.95` (`20` tiles)**, improving small root-canal-filling detection by **`+294%`**!
     - *Contrast & Super-Resolution (`pp. 6–7`):* `CLAHE` and bilateral filtering combined with ROI tiling mitigate domain shift (`p. 7`); `LTE` super-resolution (`Mohammad-Rahimi et al., 2023 [39]`, `888` OPGs at `4×` downscaling) outperformed `SRCNN`, `SRGAN`, `U-Net`, `SwinIR`, and bicubic interpolation (`p. 6`).

## 3. Clinical Acceptance, Ethics & US/EU Regulatory Synthesis (`Sections 4.3–4.4`, pp. 7–9, Fig. 3)
- **Commercial platforms in clinical use (`p. 8`):** `Second Opinion` (`Pearl Inc.`), `Diagnocat` (`Diagnocat Ltd.`), `Automate` (`3Shape A/S`), and `Smilecloud` (`Smilecloud Biometrics`).
- **Stakeholder ethics survey (`Müller et al., 2021 [27]`, `N = 102` participants, `p. 8`):** **`81.4%`** require explicit patient informed consent prior to AI analysis, and **`74.5%`** demand full transparency on health-data handling.
- **US vs. EU Regulatory Architecture for Dental AI `SaMDs` (`Section 4.4`, pp. 8–9, Fig. 3):**

| Dimension | United States (`FDA` Framework) | European Union (`EU` / `EEA` Framework) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Agency / Assessors** | **US Food and Drug Administration (`FDA`)** + local **`IRBs`** | **European Medicines Agency (`EMA`)** + Decentralized **Notified Bodies** (`CE Marking`) |
| **Device Classification & Rules** | Risk-based `Class I`, `Class II`, `Class III` (premarket approval) | **`MDR` (`EU 2017/745`)** & **`IVDR` (`EU 2017/746`)** — most diagnostic AI `SaMDs` are high-risk **`Class IIa` or higher** |
| **AI-Specific Regulation** | `2019` Proposed SaMD Framework -> `2021 AI/ML SaMD Action Plan` -> **`2023 Predetermined Change Control Plan (`PCCP`)`** for iterative ML updates | **`EU Artificial Intelligence Act (`AIA`, adopted 2024)`** (risk management, data governance, human oversight, robustness) + `DMA` & `DSA` |
| **Data Privacy & Cybersecurity** | **`HIPAA` (`1996`)** (health-specific privacy rule) | **`GDPR` (`EU 2016/679`)** + **`NIS2 Directive` (`EU 2022/2555`)** + **`Cybersecurity Act` (`EU 2019/881`, `ENISA`)** |

## 4. Limitations & Reviewer Methodological Critique (`OS §3.2`)
### 4.1 Author-Stated Limitations of Dental AI (`Sections 2.3, 4.1–4.3`, pp. 3, 6–8)
1. **Sensitivity to overlapping anatomy, artifacts, and scanner domain shift (`pp. 3–4, 6–7`):** Performance drops on external multi-center cohorts (`Mureșanu et al., 2024 [28]`) and fine qualitative tasks (e.g., `Diagnocat`'s `8.33%–14.29%` F1 on short fillings/voids).
2. **Observer & radiographer quality bias (`p. 7`):** Cites `Decoster et al. [46]`, where radiographers rated `56%` of radiographs as non-diagnostic/limited vs. `30.7%` for radiologists.
3. **Medicolegal ambiguity & fear of autonomy loss (`p. 8`):** Hinders routine clinical adoption without standardized education and regulatory clarity.

### 4.2 Reviewer Methodological Critique of `P0028` (`OS §3.2` Audit)
1. **Systematic reference-number shift in `Section 3.2` (`pp. 4–5` vs. `Table 1`, p. 7 & `References`, p. 11):**
   - On `p. 4` (line 434), `Mask R-CNN` on `1,000` Tufts OPGs (`mAP = 92.49%, Acc = 100%`) is cited as `(30)` (`Rohrer et al., 2022`), when it actually corresponds to **`(61)` (`Rubiu et al., 2023`)**.
   - On `p. 5` (line 468), the `1,781`-OPG `U-Net` `6/10/20`-tile restoration segmentation study (`F1: 0.70 -> 0.83 -> 0.92 -> 0.95`, `+294%` RCT improvement) is cited as `(31)` (`Rocha et al., 2022`), when it actually corresponds to **`(30)` (`Rohrer et al., 2022`)**.
   - On `p. 5` (line 477), the `1,500`-OPG `DoubleU-Net / Nano-Net` study (`Acc = 96.591%, Dice = 92.886%`) is cited as `(32)` (`Prados-Privado et al., 2021`), when it actually corresponds to **`(31)` (`Rocha et al., 2022`)**.
   - On `p. 5` (line 486), the `1,000`-OPG `Mask R-CNN` tooth-presence study is cited as `(33)` (`Turosz et al., 2024`), when it actually corresponds to **`(32)` (`Prados-Privado et al., 2021`)**.
2. **Swapped citations between `Kwiatek et al. (2025) [21]` and `Kazimierczak et al. (2024) [22]` (`pp. 2–3, 7, 11`):** In `Section 2.1` (`pp. 2–3`) and `Table 1` (`p. 7`), the `55`-patient `Diagnocat` endodontic filling evaluation (`90.72%` acc; `8.33%` F1 on adequate obturation; `14.29%` F1 on density) is attributed to `Kwiatek et al., 2025 (21)` (a caries paper), whereas in the Reference list (`p. 11`) that endodontic study is actually **`Kazimierczak et al., 2024 (22)`**!
3. **Conflation of distinct studies and metrics in `Table 1` (`pp. 3–4, 6–7`):**
   - `Gaudin et al., 2024 (26)` is described on `p. 3` as a `ResNet` osteoporosis model on `340` OPGs (`92.6%` acc, `>91%` spec, `<1 s/image`) and on `p. 4` as a `YOLOv8 + EfficientNet` model on `348` OPGs (`Sensitivity = 0.83, F1 = 0.53, AUC = 0.76`), and `Table 1` (`p. 7`) merges both into a single row (`348` patients, `Accuracy = 92.6%`, `F1 = 53%`).
   - `Alharbi et al., 2023 (29)` (`U-Net3+` on the `DNS` dataset) is described as `"510 images expanded to 4,296"` on `p. 4` and as `"1,500 annotated images"` on `p. 6`.
   - `Table 1` (`p. 7`) places `Küçük et al., 2025 (38)`'s `mAP@0.5 = 98.3%` under the `"Accuracy"` column, and lacks a `PRISMA` selection flow or `QUADAS-2` appraisal.
