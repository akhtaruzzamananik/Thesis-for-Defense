## 1. Problem & Clinical / Technical Motivation
- **Clinical motivation (pp. 1–2):** First-line clinical interpretation of 2D panoramic radiographs (`OPGs`) requires simultaneously identifying maxillofacial anatomical landmarks (`Maxilla`, `Mandible`, `Maxillary Sinus`, `Inferior Alveolar Nerve [IAN]`), teeth (`Tooth`, `Supernumerary`), and restorative/prosthetic/endodontic treatments (`Implant`, `Screw`, `Crown/pontic`, `Endodontic Treatment`, `Metallic Restoration`, `Resin-based Restoration`). Automated detection and segmentation can reduce observer fatigue and inter-rater variability among less experienced clinicians.
- **Technical problem (pp. 2–3):** Little empirical evidence exists on how consecutive YOLO generations (`YOLOv5`, `YOLOv7`, `YOLOv8`) compare when jointly performing **multiclass bounding-box object detection** and **polygon instance segmentation** on a moderately sized (`N = 300` to `600`), clinically realistic OPG dataset containing motion blur and orthodontic appliances (`pp. 2, 7`).
- **Objective (p. 2):** Benchmark **`YOLOv5s`**, **`YOLOv7m`**, **`YOLOv8m`**, and **`YOLOv8l`** across **12 anatomical, dental, and treatment classes** for both object detection and instance segmentation following `STARD` and `CLAIM` guidelines (`p. 2`).

## 2. Dataset, Annotators & Splits
- **Cohort & acquisition (`Section 2.1`, p. 2):**
  - **Source:** **`600` anonymized permanent-dentition panoramic radiographs** (`.jpeg`, **`2706 × 1536` pixels** at `323 × 323` dpi) randomly selected at Complutense University of Madrid, Spain (local Ethics Committee approved, `p. 2`).
  - **Scanner:** **`Kodak 9000 3D Extraoral Imaging System`** (`Carestream Health Inc.`, New York, USA) at `73 kVp`, `12 mA`, and `12.5 s` exposure (`p. 2`).
  - **Clinical heterogeneity (`pp. 2, 7`):** Intentionally retained permanent-dentition OPGs containing diverse restorative/prosthetic treatments, orthodontic appliances, and non-ideal resolution from patient movement.
  - **Two-stage sample size scaling (`p. 2, Table 1`):** Based on sample-size calculation (`n = 245.86` at `P = 0.8, d = 0.05, Z = 1.96`), an initial cohort of **`300` OPGs** was used to test `YOLOv5s`; due to poor detection metrics (`mAP0.5 = 0.360`), a second batch of **`300` OPGs** was added to reach **`N = 600` OPGs**.
- **Dataset split (`Section 2.1`, p. 2):**
  - **Training set:** `490` OPGs (`81.7%`)
  - **Validation set:** `50` OPGs (`8.3%`)
  - **Testing set:** `60` OPGs (`10.0%`)
- **Annotation protocol & observer reliability (`Section 2.2`, pp. 2–3):**
  - **Tool & effort:** Manually annotated using **`COCO Annotator v.11.0.1`** with polygon vertices and bounding boxes (`COCO .json` converted via `Pascal VOC` to `YOLO` format, `p. 3`). Average annotation time was **`15 min/OPG` (`~150 hours` total)**, yielding **`23,323` total annotations** (`mean = 38.87` annotations/OPG, `p. 3`). Per-class instance counts are `not reported`.
  - **Inter- and intra-operator reliability (`p. 2`):** Two trained clinicians (`A.H-M` and `M.B-G`) independently annotated `35` calibration OPGs, achieving **inter-rater `ICC = 0.99`** (segmentation `F1 ≥ 0.76`). One operator (`M.B-G`) then annotated all `600` OPGs and re-evaluated the `35` OPGs at `1, 3, and 6 months`, achieving **intra-rater `ICC = 1.000`**.

## 3. Methodology & Experimental Design
- **Evaluated architectures (`Section 2.3`, p. 3, Fig. 2):**
  1. **`YOLOv5s` (Small):** Evaluated on `N = 300` vs. `N = 600` for object detection (`Tables 1–3, 5, 7`); excluded from segmentation tables because its segmentation outputs were deemed non-comparable (`p. 4`).
  2. **`YOLOv7m` (Medium):** Extended Efficient Layer Aggregation Network (`E-ELAN`) backbone; evaluated on both object detection and instance segmentation (`Tables 2–8`).
  3. **`YOLOv8m` (Medium) & `YOLOv8l` (Large):** `CSPDarknet` (`C2f`) anchor-free decoupled detection and segmentation heads; evaluated on both object detection and instance segmentation (`Tables 2–8`).
- **Training hyperparameters, input resize dimensions, augmentation, hardware, and runtime (`FPS`/`ms`):** `not reported`.

### 4.4 False-Negative Confusion Analysis (`Tables 7–8`, pp. 8–9)
- **Primary source of False Negatives (`Tables 7–8`):** Across both object detection (`Table 7`) and segmentation (`Table 8`), **background misclassification (`B`)** is the dominant FN source for almost all anatomical structures (`Maxilla`, `Mandible`, `Maxillary Sinus`, `IAN`) and rare classes (`Supernumerary`, `Screw`).
- **Inter-treatment confusion (`Tables 7–8`, p. 9):** Strong mutual confusion occurs among `Metallic Restoration (MR)`, `Resin-based Restoration (RR)`, and `Crown/pontic (C/P)` (due to overlapping radiopacities and coronal positions), while `Endodontic Treatment (ET)` is frequently misclassified as `Background (B)` or `Tooth (T)`.

## 5. Limitations
### 5.1 Author-Stated Limitations (`Section 4`, p. 10)
1. **Reduced sample size (`N = 600` OPGs) & unaddressed class imbalance (`p. 10`):** Insufficient instances of rare classes (`Supernumerary` and `Screw`) caused complete or near-complete failure on those classes, and no data augmentation or class-balancing strategy was applied (`p. 10`).
2. **Single scanner & single ethnicity (`p. 10`):** All radiographs came from one `Kodak 9000 3D` unit at one Spanish university clinic, motivating future multi-center / Federated Learning validation.
3. **Poor segmentation of large bony structures and small treatments (`pp. 4, 6, 10`):** Full-OPG segmentation struggled on `Maxilla`/`Mandible` (`mAP0.5–0.95 = 0.070–0.357`) and small restorative/endodontic features, with the authors citing `Rohrer et al. [29]`'s 12-tile cropping strategy as a needed future remedy (`p. 10`).

### 5.2 Reviewer Methodological Critique (`OS §3.2`)
1. **Direct contradiction in the Discussion regarding `Screw` detection (`p. 4` vs. `Tables 3, 5` and `p. 7`):** On `p. 4` (line 344), the authors claim *"except for screw detection, where YOLOv5s performed optimally"*. However, `Table 3` (`p. 5`) and `Table 5` (`p. 6`) show that **`YOLOv5s` completely failed on `Screw` (`Recall = 0.000, mAP0.5 = 0.083, F1 = 0.000, TP = 9%, FN = 91%`)**, whereas **`YOLOv7m` achieved `Recall = 0.636, mAP0.5 = 0.680, F1 = 0.666, TP = 90%`** (as correctly noted later on `p. 7`, line 452). This error occurred because Ultralytics assigns a default `Precision = 1.000` when zero predictions are made (`R = 0.000`), which also happened for `Supernumerary` (`P = 1.000, R = 0.000, mAP0.5 = 0.000` across all models in `Tables 3–4`), artificially inflating the mean precision of models that predict nothing for rare classes!
2. **Mathematical anomalies in `Table 4` (`p. 6`) segmentation `F1` and `IoU` columns:** In `Table 4` (`p. 6`), several `F1 Score` values violate the harmonic mean `2PR / (P + R)` of the printed `P` and `R` columns:
   - `Implant` (`YOLOv7m`): `P = 0.687, R = 0.727` (`true F1 = 0.706`), yet `Table 4` prints **`F1 Score = 0.974` and `IoU = 0.949`** while `mAP0.5 = 0.598` and `mAP0.5–0.95 = 0.112`! The same happens for `YOLOv8m` (`P = 0.858, R = 0.909 -> printed F1 = 0.971, IoU = 0.944`) and `YOLOv8l` (`P = 0.853, R = 0.909 -> printed F1 = 0.962, IoU = 0.926`).
   - `Mandible` (`YOLOv8m`): `mAP0.5 = 0.285`, yet **`mAP0.5–0.95 = 0.357` (`> mAP0.5`)**, which is mathematically impossible since `mAP0.5–0.95` averages `AP` across `IoU ∈ [0.50, 0.95]` where `AP(IoU)` is monotonically non-increasing with `IoU`!
3. **Complete omission of training hyperparameters, per-class instance counts, hardware, and runtime (`G6`):** Neither the input image resolution (`640` vs. native `2706 × 1536`), training epochs, batch size, optimizer, GPU model, nor inference latency (`ms/FPS`) is reported anywhere (`not reported`).

## 6. Relevance to Our Thesis (`STEP-007` Synthesis Links)
- **Direct empirical proof of dataset-size scaling (`N = 300` -> `600`, `Table 1`) and detection vs. segmentation trade-offs (`G1` / `G4`):** Shows that doubling OPG count from `300` to `600` boosts `YOLOv5s` `mAP0.5` by `+0.271` (`0.360 -> 0.631`), and provides head-to-head `YOLOv5s vs. YOLOv7m vs. YOLOv8m/l` benchmarks across 12 anatomical and restorative classes.
- **Strong support for our thesis's tiling/ROI and background-error reduction hypotheses (`G2` / `S9`):** Demonstrates that full-OPG detection/segmentation suffers heavily from background false negatives (`Tables 7–8`) and struggles on small `Resin-based Restorations` (`mAP0.5–0.95 = 0.205–0.304`), `Endodontic Treatments` (`0.172–0.393`), and `Screws` (`0.016–0.157`), directly motivating high-resolution tiling (`Rohrer et al. [29]`, cited on `p. 10`) and contrast enhancement (`CLAHE`).
