## 1. Problem & Clinical / Technical Motivation
- **Reference-standard & MLLM evaluation gap (`pp. 1–3`):** While specialized detectors (`YOLOv8`, `YOLOv11`, `Faster R-CNN`) achieve strong performance on panoramic radiographs (`OPGs`), general-purpose Multimodal Large Language Models (`MLLMs`) have mostly been tested on text exams or clinical vignettes rather than structured tooth-level abnormality charting directly from raw OPGs. Furthermore, OPG reference standards frequently lack an auditable human-in-the-loop (`HITL`) verification workflow to correct automated `FDI` tooth-numbering errors.
- **Objectives (`p. 3`):**
  1. Develop a portable Flask-based **Human-in-the-Loop (`HITL`) OPG annotation & review platform** (`OPG-233`, `https://github.com/simzhangbest/opg233-benchmark`) with 3-panel visualization and detector-assisted `FDI` recommendation + clinician confirmation.
  2. Benchmark four MLLMs — **`M365 Copilot (GPT 5.6, Think-deeper)`**, **`Gemini-3.6-flash-medium`**, **`Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct`**, and **`doubao_web`** — on **Task 1 (tooth-level finding presence)** and **Task 2 (tooth–finding pair classification)** across a closed `20-class` vocabulary.
  3. Quantify inter-model error independence (`Cohen's κ`), cluster-robust paired significance (`McNemar` + `Wilcoxon`), and the sensitivity vs. clinician-arbitration trade-off of a two-model cross-check workflow.

## 2. HITL Platform & `OPG-233` Reference-Standard Construction (`pp. 4–7`, `Figs. 1–3`, `Tables 1–2`)
- **Corpus curation (`Table 2`, pp. 5–6):**
  - Deduplicated `23,413` exported images -> `13,932` (after removing augmentations) -> `8,674` (one per source) -> **`4,785` unique OPGs at native `1615 × 840 px`** (discarding `640 × 640` rescaled copies).
  - Combines **openly licensed Roboflow OPGs (`zaks lab "dentist xrays everything" [25]`, `4,373` images, `91.4%`)** and **institutional OPGs from Department of Stomatology, Yijishan Hospital of Wannan Medical College, Wuhu, China (`412` retained of `500` provided, `8.6%`; Ethics `2026-210`)**.
- **Blind double-expert screening (`pp. 5–7`):**
  - Screened `320` consecutive candidates (`a0001–a0320`) to admit **`233` consensus-verified OPGs** (`1` OPG/patient; **`163` public [`70.0%`] + `70` institutional [`30.0%`]**) and excluded **`87` (`27.2%`)** whenever two blinded senior oral clinicians disagreed or spotted any `FDI` or lesion-contour error (interface had no control to add missing polygons, so incomplete cases were excluded).
- **Geometric `FDI` recommendation vs. manual clinician entry (`p. 7`):**
  - A **`33-class` tooth detector trained on the Tufts Dental Database (`1,000` OPGs, `26,005` tooth boxes; held-out `mAP50 = 0.973, mAP50–95 = 0.725, Precision = 0.948, Recall = 0.957`)** proposed initial `FDI` numbers by normalized center geometry.
  - Across the **`2,205` annotated lesion instances** (`31-class` raw coding), the geometric recommendation matched the final number in **only `289 / 1,948` (`14.8%`)** (`222` [`10.1%`] accepted + `67` unconfirmed); clinicians **manually re-entered `1,685` (`76.4%`)** tooth numbers, while `298` (`13.5%`, `231` empty) remained unconfirmed.
- **Instance-to-tooth mapping & denominators (`pp. 4, 7, 9–10, 13`):**
  - Mapping `2,205` polygon instances onto permanent `FDI` teeth (`11–48`) and a closed `20-class` disease vocabulary discarded **`346` instances (`15.7%`)** — including `231` unnumbered instances and **`91%` of `Bone loss` regions (`222 -> 20` pairs)**, `Wire` (`17 -> 1`), `Metal band` (`16 -> 4`), and `Orthodontic brackets` (`2 -> 0`).
  - Final benchmark denominators across `233` OPGs (`32 × 233 = 7,456` permanent tooth positions): **`1,859` tooth–finding pairs** across **`1,600` annotated tooth positions (`1,599` scorable positive tooth positions** after dropping `1` unnumbered entry, and **`5,857` negative tooth positions**).

## 3. MLLM Benchmarking Protocol & Quantitative Results (`pp. 7–14`, `Tables 3–9`, `Figs. 4–5`)
- **Two unmatched access arms (`Table 3`, pp. 7–8; evaluated 10–11 Aug 2026):**
  - **API Arm (`temp = 0.1, max_tokens = 8192, max_image_side = 1280 px, JPEG quality = 85`):** `Gemini-3.6-flash-medium` (Google) and `Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct` (Alibaba, open-weight).
  - **Consumer Web Arm (uncontrolled server-side compression/temp/seed):** `M365 Copilot (GPT 5.6, Think-deeper mode)` (Microsoft, file upload) and `doubao_web` (ByteDance, browser paste).
  - **Unified bilingual prompt (`p. 8`):** English instructions + Chinese `20-class` disease vocabulary (`27-entry` synonym alias normalization table) enforcing a single structured JSON output per OPG.

### 3.3 Cluster-Robust Paired Tests, Error Independence & Two-Model Cross-Check (`Tables 6–8`, pp. 10–12)
- **Within-arm & cross-arm significance (`Tables 6–7`):** Within the Web arm, `M365` beat `doubao_web` (`Wilcoxon p = 1.9e-13; cluster-robust McNemar p = 0.002, Holm p = 0.0058`); within the API arm, `Gemini-3.6` beat `Qwen3-VL-32B` (`Wilcoxon p = 4.1e-07; cluster-robust McNemar p = 1.2e-12, Holm p = 6.0e-12`). Across arms, `M365` vs. `Gemini-3.6` was borderline on per-case `F1` (`Wilcoxon p = 0.052`, means `0.637` vs. `0.618`) but significant on tooth-level concordance (`523` `M365`-only vs. `371` `Gemini`-only correct teeth; `pooled p = 4.15e-07`, `cluster-robust p = 0.005`, `Holm p = 0.010`).
- **Intra-OPG clustering design effect (`p. 10`):** Ratio of case-level cluster-robust sandwich variance to naive tooth-independence variance was **`3.05–6.17`** (`1.7–2.5×` SE inflation).
- **Inter-model error independence (`p. 12`):** `M365–Gemini` showed partial error agreement (**`κ = 0.316`**), whereas all other 5 pairs had **negative $\kappa$** (`M365–Qwen = -0.007, Gemini–Qwen = -0.040, M365–doubao = -0.041, Gemini–doubao = -0.058, Qwen–doubao = -0.203`).
- **Two-model cross-check trade-off (`Table 8`, p. 12):** Pairing models boosted tooth-level **union recall** from `0.668` (`M365` alone) to **`0.764` (`Gemini + M365`, `Intersection recall = 0.560`, requiring human arbitration on `326 / 1,599 = 20.4%` of positive teeth)** or **`0.854` (`Qwen + Gemini`, `Intersection recall = 0.565`, requiring arbitration on `462 / 1,599 = 28.9%` of positive teeth)**.

## 4. Limitations & Reviewer Methodological Critique (`OS §3.2`)
### 4.1 Author-Stated Limitations (`Section 7`, p. 15)
1. **Merged public + institutional corpus without per-image source tag in exported files (`p. 15`).**
2. **Spectrum / selection bias (`27.2%` exclusion rate, `pp. 5–6, 15`):** Because the review platform lacked a tool to draw missing lesion polygons, `87 / 320` (`27.2%`) candidate OPGs with incomplete/disagreed annotations were excluded outright, enriching `OPG-233` for unambiguous cases.
3. **Unmatched API vs. Consumer Web arms & single-run evaluation (`pp. 8, 15`):** Web interfaces (`M365`, `doubao_web`) apply undisclosed server-side image compression and expose no temperature/seed, preventing multi-run variance estimation or strict cross-arm ranking.
4. **No independent human-reader benchmark (`p. 15`).**

### 4.2 Reviewer Methodological Critique (`OS §3.2` Audit)
1. **Severe Tooth-Mapping Attrition (`15.7%` overall, `91%` for Bone Loss) & Geometry-Based `FDI` Recommender Failure (`85.2%` rejection/correction rate, `pp. 4, 7, 13`):**
   - Although the Tufts-trained `33-class` tooth detector achieved `mAP50 = 0.973` on Tufts, its normalized-center geometry heuristic matched the clinicians' final `FDI` number on **only `14.8%` (`289 / 1,948`)** of findings in `OPG-233`, forcing manual clinician entry on **`76.4%` (`1,685 / 2,205`)** of findings!
   - Furthermore, `346 / 2,205` (`15.7%`) annotated polygons — including `231` unconfirmed/unnumbered findings and `202 / 222` (`91.0%`) `Bone loss` regions — were dropped when mapping polygons to tooth numbers, distorting the ground-truth prevalence of diffuse/multi-tooth conditions.
2. **No Head-to-Head Object Detector Baseline (`YOLO` / `RT-DETR`) on the Same `233` OPGs:** While the Background cites `YOLOv8` (`[1]`) and `YOLOv11/v12` (`Mutlu et al., 2026 [2]` = our `P0015`) reaching `mAP@0.5 = 0.857`, the authors did not run any supervised detector on `OPG-233` to quantify the massive gap between dedicated vision detectors and zero-shot MLLMs (`Caries F1 = 0.000–0.075`, `Periapical lesion F1 = 0.000–0.023`).
3. **Arbitration Cost Denominator Omit False Positives on Negative Teeth (`Table 8`, p. 12 vs. `Table 5`, p. 9):** `Table 8` computes the `"One model only"` arbitration rate (`20.4%–42.3%`) solely as a fraction of the `1,599` *reference-standard positive teeth*, ignoring the **`485` to `1,716` false-positive teeth (`Table 5`)** predicted on the `5,857` negative tooth positions that a clinician in a real pre-screening union workflow would also have to inspect and reject!

## 5. Relevance to Our Thesis (`STEP-007` Synthesis Links)
- **Empirical proof that zero-shot MLLMs cannot replace dedicated OPG object detectors (`G1`):** Even frontier 2026 MLLMs (`GPT 5.6`, `Gemini-3.6-flash`, `Qwen3-VL-32B`) achieve **`F1 < 0.08` on `Caries` (`n = 166`) and `< 0.03` on `Periapical lesions` (`n = 61`)** and overall pair `F1 <= 0.485`, proving the necessity of dedicated localized detectors (`YOLOv8–v12 / RT-DETR` with high-resolution tiling/CLAHE).
- **Blueprint for our Annotation QA & Statistical Evaluation (`G3 / G4 / G6`):**
  1. Validates our **Human-in-the-Loop (`HITL`) two-stage annotation review protocol**, showing why automated geometric `FDI` assignment (`14.8%` raw agreement on lesions) must be paired with manual clinician verification.
  2. Demonstrates why **case-level cluster-robust bootstrap (`2,000` replicates) and sandwich-variance `McNemar` tests** are mandatory in OPG studies (naive tooth-level tests underestimate variance by a design effect of **`3.05–6.17×`**).
